{# THREE BUCKETS, NAMED, BECAUSE ONE OF THEM IS WHAT SHE WILL ACTUALLY HIT The earlier wording promised a priced budget plus a list of things with no cost. That describes two of the three states and omits the one an assessment usually lands in today: a real researched rate -- $5.75 per square foot for repaint, say -- with no measured area to apply it to. Nobody records areas on the walk yet, so on a freshly-walked unit that bucket can be ALL of it, and the budget opens with no total. That is correct and it is the point of the rate fix. But a person who was promised "prices the items needing work" and gets a budget totalling nothing concludes the tool is broken. So the sentence says all three states plainly, before the file is opened. #}

The capital budget covers the items recorded as needing work, and separates three things: what it can price, what has a researched rate but still needs a measurement (an area or a length) before it can be totalled, and what has no researched cost at all. Items in the last two are listed with the reason, and are not in the total.
